# How to actually use this

## The rules that matter

**1. Stop at 45 minutes.** Not because the task is finished - because a habit that fits in your
life survives, and one that does not, does not. If the Build block overruns, read the approach
section in the brief, note what you would have done, and move on. Days you did not finish are
still days you learned.

**2. Never skip the Reflect block.** Ten minutes of writing does more for retention than twenty
more minutes of coding. The prompt in each brief asks for the *trade-off*, because that is the
form the knowledge takes when you need it in an interview or a design review.

**3. Type the code.** Do not paste solutions. The 25-minute block is calibrated for typing.

**4. Commit every day.** One commit, message `day NN: <what you learned>`. In three months the
log is a map of your own understanding.

## When you get stuck

In order:

1. Re-read the failing test. It names exactly what is expected.
2. Re-read the **Build** section - the constraint you are missing is usually stated there.
3. Read the **Approach** section at the end of the brief. It is not a solution, it is the shape.
4. Still stuck at 25 minutes? Write down *what specifically confuses you* in `NOTES.md` and move
   on. Come back at the phase review. Confusion you have named is much easier to resolve later.

## Spaced repetition, cheaply

At each phase review (days 10, 20, 30 ...), reread your `NOTES.md` from that phase before
starting. Five minutes. This is the single highest-leverage habit in the whole programme,
because system design knowledge decays fast without retrieval practice.

## If you have more than 45 minutes

Do the **Stretch** section, not the next day. Depth on one idea beats breadth across two.
The schedule is not a race; the twenty-first day is not better than the seventh.

## If you miss days

Missing one day is fine. Missing two in a row is the risk point - that is where programmes die.
If you miss a week, do **not** try to catch up by doubling. Resume at the next day and let the
calendar slip. Ninety sessions is the commitment, not ninety consecutive dates.

## Adapting the curriculum

All ninety days are written. If a phase turns out to be pitched wrong for you - too much
hand-holding, not enough, or the wrong emphasis - say so and it can be regenerated around what you
actually found hard.
